Field mapping (UAE MoF mandatory fields v1.1)

The required fields above (*) implement the official UAE Electronic Invoice Mandatory Fields (Ministry of Finance, v1.1, 28 Oct 2026). Covered: 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 24, 26–29, 30–34, 39–51. Prefilled or derived in this demo: 5 (transaction flags), 11–12 (your e-invoice address and identifier, derived from your TRN), 20 (seller country).

Invoice register

Every invoice you check is recorded here — and an accepted invoice is never downgraded by a later failed attempt. All statuses are simulated — no money has been collected.

What happens after acceptance (concept)

The idea: compliance is not a portal visit, it is the first step of getting paid. An accepted invoice would move into the receivables workflow — collections, payment matching, books updated.
Invoice accepted (simulated)→ Collections follow-up→ Payment matched · books updated (proposed, not built)
In this prototype the accepted status and its next step are shown in the register above; the collections workflow itself is described in the architecture document.

Production notes (failure modes and security)

Failure modes in production: provider timeout (queue + retry with backoff), negative MLS (field-level diagnosis surfaces on the invoice), TDD report failure (hold invoicing state, alert), ERP sync conflict (write-back reconciliation). Security: least-privilege API credentials per provider, immutable audit log of submissions, PII minimisation (buyer endpoint IDs only).
